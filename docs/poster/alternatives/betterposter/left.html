Why this matters

The edge of a wildfire is the most dangerous place for a firefighter. Robot dogs (four-legged robots) can climb steep, rocky, smoky ground where trucks get stuck and drones can't see.

But a robot near a fire must know where the fire will be, not just where it is now.

How we forecast the fire

Real fire history~246,000 NASA satellite fire detections from 30 major wildfires worldwide
Conditionsweather (wind, humidity, temperature, rain), terrain (elevation, slope), land cover, and the fire's current shape
Machine learning (XGBoost)27 inputs per 50 m cell. Trained on 24 fires, tested on 6 it never saw.
Physics (Rothermel spread equation)fuel × wind × slope × dryness; the ML model adjusts the rate
Outputthe chance each 50 m cell catches fire, every 15-minute step

The simulator

Real 3D terrain near Big Sur, Monterey County, California (summer weather). Runs in a web browser on Google 3D map tiles.

Is the forecast any good?

Tested on 6 unseen fires (USA, Bolivia, Siberia, Greece). 91% of cases there are "fire spreads," so the baseline always guesses "spreads."
